CONNECTION

Network and Tor

Tor disabledTor routes only Nacre traffic. It is not a system VPN. By default, your ISP sees a connection to the Tor network; it may also see timing and volume.

Tor will try to use the requested exit country, but the country used may differ. Websites may infer your location from other signals.

Pages and iframes use en-US/UTC in tests. navigator.languages still differs in some workers; do not consider all language surfaces identical.

A site normally sees the Tor exit relay IP address. Your ISP and the first relay still know your origin IP. Some sites block Tor. Snowflake also contacts its broker and STUN/WebRTC servers; Snowflake volunteers relay encrypted data intended for the Tor network, not your traffic in clear text.

Direct mode: sites receive your public IP and your ISP may observe destination IPs and metadata. Chromium secure DNS is configured, but absence of system DNS fallback and ECH have not been verified by packet capture.

NACRE 1.1.0 · BETA

About

Nacre does not persist browsing history, but keeps up to eight recently closed tabs in memory. Cookies, cache, and site storage in the normal profile persist unless you clear them or enable “Clear site data on close”. Downloaded files and recently closed tabs are not removed automatically.

Check for updates without Tor?Without Tor, GitHub sees your IP address when Nacre checks for updates. Nothing else is sent.

With Tor, every update request goes through Tor. Without Tor, Nacre asks once before contacting GitHub directly. Each update is checked against Nacre's signing key before it is installed; GitHub never receives your history or bookmarks.

The Electron fixture NetLog observed a Chromium IPv6 UDP attempt to [2001:4860:4860::8888]:443, including with local SOCKS and a closed proxy. It does not prove packets were actually sent on the Windows interface. The absence of parallel output in the installed app is therefore unverified.
